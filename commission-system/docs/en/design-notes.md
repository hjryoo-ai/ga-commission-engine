# Design Notes on the GA Commission System Design Document (Reflecting Implementation Results)

> Subject: `commission-system/docs/설계서.md` (v1.2.1 — **single source of truth**) / Implementation: `commission-system/` (Gradle multi-module, **Phase 0–16 complete — up to a runnable app**, 356 tests passing = unit/H2 · app smoke 254 + Oracle integration 102)
> Written: 2026-07-16 (last updated 2026-07-17, Phase 11 + consolidation of documents into a single source)
>
> Note: The single source of the design document is now the repository's `commission-system/docs/설계서.md` (v1.1.5), and the "proposals for the design document" in §6/§6b/§8 below have been **folded into v1.1.5** (§3.0 summation convention, §7 separation of close/payout, Appendix B batch conventions 14/15, §5.9 force_reason, etc.). ## 1. Overall Assessment of the Design Document

The four first principles of the design (rules = data, absorbing change through new Steps, immutable ledger + reversal entries, BigDecimal + central rounding) were all sound and could be carried through unchanged in the actual implementation. In particular, the following two judgments greatly lowered implementation difficulty.

- **The "reference date is mandatory, no currently-effective lookup API" convention** — Because every lookup in RuleRepository is forced to take a reference date, the 2026-06-30/07-01 boundary cases resolved naturally as "no rule data means not applied," without separate branching code. Effective-date branches such as the expansion of the 1200% rule and the introduction of deferral were all reduced to data problems.
- **General-purpose rule engine not recommended (3-tier hybrid)** — Rates/curves/clawback rates are tables, policy branches are parameters (enums/flags), and calculation structure is versioned Steps. Tests proved that extending deferral from 4 years to 7 years takes nothing more than adding 2 curve records.

## 2. Decisions Settled in Implementation on Points the Design Document Left Open

During implementation, points not specified in the design document were settled as follows. The rationale for each is left as a comment in the code.

1. **Summation convention (important)**: REVERSED originals are **not excluded** from summation; negative reversal records cancel them out. Unlike the "exclude the original" approach, close-month immutability (past numbers do not change) and payout/clawback accounting are unified under one convention, and corrections to originals in PAID status are handled by the same formula. The clawback base amount (net amount already paid) calculation also follows this convention.
2. **When the limit ledger is posted**: LimitGateStep only makes the gate decision (cut/defer); posting to the ledger is done by a listener (`LimitLedgerPoster`) right after COMM_CALC is saved, **with the confirmed calcId**. Because of the integrity of LIMIT_LEDGER_DTL's basis (calc_id), posting before the save is impossible — the design document's §4.3 CalcContext alone does not solve this timing problem, so a "post-save hook" (`CalcResultListener`) step was added. **We propose that the design document state this step explicitly.**
3. **Restoring the limit on reversal entries**: Handled not by re-deciding per type but by **inverting the sign of the amount posted in DTL under the original calcId** (`LimitReversalHook`). It is exact, and unaffected even if the set of limit-included types changes.
4. **Deferral × limit interaction**: The limit is consumed by the **total amount before splitting** (§2.2 "the schedule itself is an output of the limit"), and DEFERRED payouts falling due do not pass through the gate again. Implemented with Step order = LimitGate(50) → DeferralSplit(55).
5. **Deferral rounding**: Each point is allocated with truncation + the last point absorbs the remainder → the invariant "sum of schedule = deferred principal" holds for arbitrary amounts (property test, 100 runs).
6. **Version replacement semantics**: On approval, the existing ACTIVE is either trimmed (ended on the day before the start date) or SUPERSEDEd (fully covered). **Approvals that would split the period of an existing version are explicitly rejected** — this keeps the invariant "no overlapping ACTIVE for the same key" simple.
7. **Two layers of recalculation idempotency**: At the record level (preventing duplicate reversals for the same original), the engine guarantees it through the REVERSED status check; at the **value level** (running twice gives the same net amount per recipient × type), tests prove it. Dedup per batch request was separated out as the responsibility of the batch layer (request ID).
8. **Precondition for replay**: Reproducing limit gate lines requires "the ledger state at calculation time." This was implemented by reconstructing the ledger from the sum of postings in LIMIT_LEDGER_DTL prior to the target calc and feeding it into the pipeline. **We propose that this reconstruction step be codified in the audit response procedure.**

## 4. Two Intentional Deviations from the Design Document

1. **ClawbackOffsetStep (offset inside the pipeline) → moved to a close-stage service**: The process described in §6.3 ("offset against the current month's payout → turn the shortfall into a receivable → automatically offset in subsequent payouts") belongs to the close, when the monthly net amount is confirmed, not to the time of an individual calculation. Keeping it as a pipeline Step risks double offsetting, so it was moved to `ClawbackOffsetService` (called at close step ③).
2. **Withholding tax Step → payout stage**: The design document itself states it "may be performed at the payout stage," and taxing on the net amount (after offset) is correct, so it is calculated in `PayoutService`.

## 5. Environment Constraints and Responses

- This machine has no Docker, so **Flyway migrations and the schema were verified on in-memory H2 (MODE=Oracle)**. Oracle-specific elements (partitioning, `IS JSON` checks, partial unique indexes) are noted in migration comments and must be supplemented with Oracle deployment scripts. In CI, set up Testcontainers (oracle-free) as the design document specifies.
- The "ACTIVE partial unique" on `COMM_RATE` is enforced by the approval workflow (application layer) rather than DDL, for portability. On Oracle, we recommend doubling it with a function-based unique index and keeping an Ambiguous detection report in the close batch alongside it.

## 6. Phase 10 (Oracle Integration) Completion Record — 2026-07-17

Phase 10 of design document v1.1.1 §10 was completed in the directed order (① Testcontainers → ② contract tests → ③ adapters). 212 tests in total (unit/H2 157 + Oracle integration 55), 0 failures.

**Evidence that the completion criteria are met:**
- **Contract tests (§8.7)**: Abstract suites for the 9 Store ports are defined in each module's testFixtures (`contract/` package) → the in-memory reference passes first → the Oracle adapters (MyBatis) pass the same suites. Coverage: immutable ledger (rejection of calcId re-insertion, status transition rules), event_key/statement_key idempotency, posting order (posting_seq ordering), round-trip of the accum>limit state after a reduction, empty string = NULL semantics.
- **Concurrency contention (§8.6)**: On real Oracle, ① 2 concurrent calculations on the same ledger with remaining limit for only 1 → the later transaction sees the updated accum and cuts exactly 371,000 won, final accum==limit (3,600,000) ② concurrent creation of a new ledger → converges to a single ledger via retry on uq_limit violation ③ FOR UPDATE wait (blocking until commit measured) ④ on post-save hook failure, the event, calculation and posting are all rolled back (§4.2 [3.5]–[5.5] atomicity).
- **fail-fast (§6.1.4)**: Double validation — immediate rejection of a null over_limit_action in the LimitRule constructor (model layer) + LIMIT_RULE NOT NULL (data layer). Tests pass.
- **Oracle-specific DDL (V100)**: IS JSON checks, COMM_RATE ACTIVE function-based unique index, COMM_CALC RANGE partitioning, posting_seq PK — all verified by real-DB tests.

**Design document feedback (proposed for v1.1.2):**
1. The `INTERVAL` partitioning in §5.5 is **impossible on close_ym VARCHAR2(6)** (Oracle interval partitioning only allows NUMBER/DATE keys). Implemented as RANGE + MAXVALUE, with yearly partition additions managed by a DBA procedure (SPLIT). If INTERVAL is truly required, close_ym must be changed to NUMBER(6).
2. posting_seq numbering is implemented as "MAX+1 while holding the ledger lock" — the adapter's save() incrementally saves only the tail (new postings) of the aggregate loaded with the lock, so there is fundamentally no numbering contention.
3. **The Oracle adapters for the rule master (RuleRepository) and agents/organizations (AgentDirectory) were intentionally excluded from this scope.** They are read-only master data with no locking/idempotency risk, and it is right to implement them as one piece with the approval workflow (RateApprovalService's CommRateAdminStore). The concurrency tests were run with a combination of in-memory rules + Oracle transactional stores, and the contended targets (ledger, events, calculations) are all on the real DB.

## 6b. Phase 10b (Rule Master · Organization Adapters) Completion Record — 2026-07-17

Phase 10b of design document v1.1.2 §10 is complete. 259 tests in total (unit/H2 179 + Oracle integration 80), 0 failures. The existing 212 are intact.

**Evidence that the completion criteria are met:**
- **Contract tests**: Abstract suites for RuleRepository's 8 lookups, the CommRateAdminStore approval workflow and AgentDirectory are defined in testFixtures `contract/`, passing identically in-memory → Oracle. Effective-period boundary semantics pinned down: rates switch to the new version **from the day of** apply_from (the old version runs through the previous day), and grade/affiliation also switch to the new version **from the day of** the change date — proving the inclusion/exclusion rules are identical in both implementations.
- **Approval concurrency (§6.6)**: ① Deterministic interleaving — the winner transaction's commit is delayed with a latch to force open the window in which the loser's application-layer check cannot see the winner, proving that the loser violates ux_comm_rate_active → retries (re-fetch latest, re-validate) → converges to the winner via SUPERSEDE (asserting a final state that could not occur without this path). ② Barrier contention — whichever side wins, exactly 1 ACTIVE. In both cases, a single-row lookup without Ambiguous on any reference date before or after the boundary (0 overlapping ACTIVE). Retries are capped at 3, followed by explicit rejection (no silent convergence).
- **Trimming audit (§6.6)**: The audit record is **derived automatically from the replace diff in the repository layer**, not the service (TRIM/SUPERSEDE/ACTIVATE/DISCARD) — a missed record is structurally impossible. COMM_RATE_CHANGE_HIST (V8) records before/after apply_to, status, approver and time; the contention test confirms that the loser's duplicate trimming is a no-op diff and does not pollute the history (exactly 1 TRIM).
- **Reference date mandatory (Appendix B-3)**: "No find API that selects a single row by business key without taking a LocalDate" is continuously verified by a reflection-based contract test (ID lookup and full version history are on the allowlist).
- **fail-fast (Appendix B-13)**: Overlapping effective versions → AmbiguousRuleException (including version overlaps in rate and clawback tables); a missing rule returns empty and is escalated by the caller (no silent default).

**Items settled during implementation (proposed for the design document):**
1. **AGENT_ORG_HIST PK correction (V8)**: (agent_id, apply_from) cannot hold the 3-row team + branch + division chain, so it was changed to (agent_id, org_id, apply_from). Needs to be reflected in the §5 auxiliary table sketch.
2. **Rate ID numbering**: rate_id is identity (BY DEFAULT), so a separate COMM_RATE_ADMIN_SEQ (START 1000000) is used for the approval workflow — avoiding collisions with the seed/manual ranges. Recommend codifying this as an operations convention.
3. **Approver propagation path**: RateApprovalService.approve(rateId, approvedBy) overload + CommRateAdminStore.replace(rule, changedBy). The existing approve(rateId) delegates with "system" (backward compatible).

## 8. Phase 11 (Spring Batch Wiring) Completion Record — 2026-07-17

Design document v1.1.4 §6.3–6.4, §7, §10 Phase 11 and Appendix B were implemented as directed. The new module `commission-batch` (Spring Batch 5.2.6, Boot BOM 3.5.16) wraps the 4 services MonthClose/Payout/DeferralRelease/recalculation as jobs. 278 tests in total (unit/H2 192 + Oracle integration 86), 0 failures. The existing 259 are intact (excluding 3 intentional rewrites to fit the §7 payout run separation — see the defect record below).

**Evidence that the completion criteria are met (all real-Oracle ITs):**
- **Value idempotency across interruption/restart + per-item transaction atomicity** (`OracleRecalcRestartIT`): Of 4 recalculations for a retroactive rate 7.0→6.5, an `Error` (kill simulation) in the reversal hook while processing the 3rd event → job FAILED. Events 1 and 2 completed rebook (net 1,755,000 / ledger 1,755,000); **for the interrupted event 3, the item transaction was rolled back as a whole**, leaving 1 record (CALCULATED) and ledger 1,890,000 unchanged (no partial reversal, §6.1.6). Resubmitting the same `request_id` = restart → resumes from the progress offset (CTX_INDEX), no reprocessing of events 1 and 2, net 1,755,000 across all policies, exactly 12 records per recipient (original + reversal + rebook), ledger invariant holds. Resubmission after completion is DEDUP_NOOP (0 record growth).
- **Request ID dedup** (§6.4, common to all three jobs): Spring Batch `JobInstance` (job name × identifying parameters) is used as the request store. `BatchRequestRunner` maps a completed instance → no-op and a failed instance → restart. The identifying parameters are the recalculation `request_id`, the payout run `(close_ym, run_seq)`, the close `close_ym`, and the deferral `close_ym`.
- **Close gate / forced close** (`OracleMonthCloseJobIT`): Unprocessed PENDING events exist → job FAILED, month stays OPEN. A forced close (authorized user, reason) restarts the same instance by changing non-identifying parameters (force/force_reason) → COMPLETED · CLOSED · CONFIRMED, and **the reason is persisted as a checklist in BATCH_JOB_EXECUTION_CONTEXT** (meets the requirement to record authorization + reason; row existence verified by SQL).
- **3 close reports** (non-blocking safety net, same IT): ① rule data completeness — detects overlapping ACTIVE rates for the same key (rate 900001/900002) and a missing COMM_TYPE_MST (FY_COMM), ② MAXVALUE partition loading — detects that close_ym 202901, outside the yearly partitions, was loaded into pmax (§5.5), ③ approval contention — detects the close trace of an ACTIVATE (approver A) immediately followed by a SUPERSEDE (approver B) (§6.6). The job is COMPLETED even with report findings (does not block the close); finding strings are persisted in the job context.
- **Payout run repeated twice (offset and withholding tax correct)** (`OraclePayoutRunJobIT`): After seeding a 2,000,000 receivable for agent A, a save failure for A's settlement row is injected in run 1 → **because this point is after the offset (consume), the item transaction rollback restores the receivable balance to 2,000,000** (demonstrating §6.1.6 atomicity); only the 3 organization rows are settled, A stays CONFIRMED, ExitStatus=COMPLETED_WITH_SKIPS and the quarantine list is recorded in the context. In run 2 (fault cleared), only A is picked up (runSeq 2, the full net 1,890,000 offset against the receivable → payout 0). In run 1 for new September policies, the remaining 110,000 receivable is offset, and withholding tax is calculated within the run **on the net payout after offset** (income tax 3% total 59,700, local income tax 0.3% 5,970, actual payout 1,924,330 verified via the step context). Resubmitting the same run is DEDUP_NOOP; a run with no targets is harmless (settled 0).
- **Net amount calculation goes through the component** (criterion added in v1.1.4): `NetAmountCalculator` (commission-calc `net/`) was extracted as a shared component, and `PayoutService.settleOne` goes through `NetAmountCalculator.netOf(records)`. The double-deduction regression is pinned down **at the component level** (`NetAmountCalculatorTest`): with a REVERSED original + reversal + rebook coexisting, 1,755,000 (explicitly ruling out the −135,000 a status-filtered SUM would give), only dimension filters (type/recipient) allowed, record-set cancellation (1,890,000−1,323,000=567,000).

**Avoiding the chunk transaction × limit lock conflict (the point the user flagged as needing the most care):**
Jobs that include calculation/payout (recalculation, payout run) **set the step transaction manager to `ResourcelessTransactionManager` and repeat the tasklet as `CONTINUABLE`, opening one business transaction via `TransactionTemplate` (itemTx) per iteration**. For recalculation the transaction boundary is 1 event; for the payout run it is 1 recipient. A structure in which a chunk holds the limit lock across multiple items is ruled out at the source, and [3.5]–[5.5] closes within the item (the lock-holding span is limited to the item). The atomic rollback in the recalculation IT above proves that per-item atomicity holds regardless of chunk size.

**Criteria for full abort vs. item quarantine (proposed for Appendix B):**
- **Business exception (`RuntimeException`)** = quarantine the item and continue. The quarantine list is recorded as CSV in the step/job context, ExitStatus=COMPLETED_WITH_SKIPS. (e.g., an event lookup failure in recalculation, a transfer error in the payout run)
- **Infrastructure failure (`Error`)** = full abort → restart by resubmitting with the same parameters. (process kill, OOM)
- **Close / deferral RELEASE** = full abort of a single transaction (month atomicity, idempotent state transitions). Partial close / partial release is not allowed.

**Latent defect found during implementation (a real-world case for design document §3.0):**
While relocating the payout run, we found that the old `settleMonth`'s net amount calculation was **filtering only CALCULATED status and SUMming**. When a correction occurs before the close and a REVERSED original + negative reversal + rebook coexist, the status-filtered SUM counts only the rebook and misses the original and reversal, causing **double deduction (−135,000 instead of the correct 1,755,000)**. It was a latent defect the existing tests did not cover. The cause was a violation of the **§3.0 summation convention ("net = sum over all statuses, no status-filtered SUM, only via NetAmountCalculator")**; it was fixed with all-status summation in NetAmountCalculator and pinned down with component/E2E regression tests (`마감_전_정정은_이중_차감_없이_정산된다`).

**Proposals for the design document → folded into v1.1.5 (2026-07-17):**  All 5 items below were incorporated into `docs/설계서.md` v1.1.5.
1. **Codify the §3.0 summation convention** — "net = sum over all statuses, no status-filtered SUM, via NetAmountCalculator" into §0 principle 5, §3.0 and Appendix B-4. Since it is now **enforced** by the component (resolving the "component enforcement not implemented" item that remained in §7), the code backs the convention. → **Reflected in v1.1 (§3.0)**
2. **§7 separation of close/payout** — close = gate + hooks + limit verification + CONFIRMED + CLOSED only. Payout run = offset → withholding tax (on net payout after offset) → PAID, **repeatable N times per month** in units of `(close_ym × run_seq)`. → **Reflected in v1.1 (§4.2, §6.3, §7)**
3. **Appendix B batch conventions** — the per-item transaction pattern, failure quarantine criteria (RuntimeException = quarantine / Error = abort / close · RELEASE = full abort), JobInstance-based request dedup, Flyway management of the BATCH_* meta tables (auto-initialization disabled). → **Folded into v1.1.5 (Appendix B-14 · B-15, §4.2, §6.1.6, §6.4, §9)** (the v1.1.5 change history stated it as "recorded" but the body of Appendix B had no entry; it was promoted this time)
4. **AGENT_SETTLEMENT.run_seq (V9)** — a column (+ index) distinguishing payout run rounds within the same close month. → **Folded into v1.1.5 (§5 auxiliary tables)**
5. **State the 3 close reports as outputs of the §7 close procedure** — a non-blocking safety net (job COMPLETED), but findings are persisted in the job context. → **Folded into v1.1.5 (§7)**

- **Architecture tests via source scanning**: ArchUnit increases the supply-chain surface (guava/slf4j, etc.), so it was replaced with a zero-dependency source scan that targets only the precise §3.0 violation (status-filtered SUM).

## 10. Phase 13 (Incentive Condition Expression Engine) Completion Record — 2026-07-17

The incentive condition expression engine was implemented based on design document §3.6, §5.10 and §6.6. 333 tests in total (unit/H2 237 + Oracle integration 96), 0 failures. The existing 292 are intact. In this Phase the system gained, **for the first time, a surface where "user input (admin-registered condition expressions) is executed,"** and sealing that surface in a sandbox was the top priority.

**Security — SpEL sandbox (most important):**
- Condition expressions are evaluated only inside `SimpleEvaluationContext.forReadOnlyDataBinding()`. This context **rejects all** type references (`T(...)`), bean references (`@bean`), constructors (`new`), method calls and assignment, allowing only property access + SpEL operators (comparison/logical/arithmetic/matches). SpEL types are not exposed outside the `IncentiveConditionEvaluator` boundary (implementation scope).
- **Rejection of forbidden constructs is pinned down by tests** (20 kinds): asserts that `T(java.lang.Runtime).getRuntime().exec(...)`, `new ProcessBuilder(...).start()`, `@systemProperties`, `''.getClass()`, method calls on property results, assignment, etc. are all rejected with `InvalidConditionException`. This demonstrates that "the condition registration screen does not become a code execution channel."
- **Input whitelist**: All a condition expression can see are the JavaBean getters of `IncentiveConditionInput` — policy/event/agent attributes + a pre-aggregated performance snapshot (fycSum/contractCount/persistencyBp). Time, randomness and external lookups are out of reach. Unaggregated performance is exposed as 0, so decisions are deterministic with no null or NPE.

**Determinism and reproducibility:**
- Condition expressions are pure functions — the same input and the same expression always give the same result (pinned by tests). The incentive version actually used in the calculation is recorded in `rule_versions` (`INCENTIVE_MST/incentiveId=..,v=..`), and every condition decision (which incentive, true/false, amount) is left in `calc_trace` → §6.5 replay can reproduce which incentive version made which decision and how.

**fail-fast (no silent skipping):**
- At registration/approval time, parsing + trial evaluation on representative input weeds out unsuitable or dangerous condition expressions (approval rejected). Evaluation errors at calculation time are thrown and the calculation is rejected — tests confirm that the calculation-time sandbox also rejects a dangerous condition expression (ACTIVE) inserted directly, bypassing approval validation. There is no path that silently passes or skips a condition expression.

**Reuse of the rate approval pattern (fulfilling §6.6 v1.1.3 "apply the same to other masters"):**
- `IncentiveApprovalService` (trimming/SUPERSEDE, rejection of splitting approvals, overlap invariant), `IncentiveChangeEntry.diff` (automatic derivation of TRIM/SUPERSEDE/ACTIVATE/DISCARD) and `IncentiveAdminStore` are implemented isomorphically to rates. The overlap invariant applies only to **the same `incentive_cd`** (incentives with different codes are applied on top of each other). The in-memory reference and the Oracle adapter pass **the same contract** (`IncentiveApprovalContract`) — the time and approver in the change history are checked with real Oracle SQL.

**Incorporation into the calculation (existing Steps unmodified, Appendix B-12):**
- The existing `IncentiveStep` (V1, receives event attributes) is kept, and `IncentiveV2Step` (master condition evaluation) is newly registered. Activation switches through Step configuration (effective period) — V1 naturally goes dormant when there is no `incentive_amount` attribute. The order `IncentiveV2(30) < LimitGate(50)` is verified with a spy Step (incentive lines are created ahead of the limit gate and so are included in the 1200% limit).

**Design judgments:**
- **Inputs as typed getters, not an open Map**: An open `perf['key']` map yields null → evaluation exception on missing keys, which undermines determinism. Fixed typed getters (defaulting to 0 when unaggregated) secure determinism. New performance metrics are added as getters in later Phases (the performance aggregation pipeline itself is out of this scope).
- **Formulas limited to FIXED/PREMIUM_RATE in the first scope**: Focus on the condition engine (the security core); performance-linked formulas and the like extend via new `PayoutKind`s (existing kinds unmodified).
- **spring-expression introduced into `commission-rule`**: This is the first external dependency of this pure module, but SpEL is encapsulated in `implementation` scope, and the artifact was already in verification-metadata (transitive from the Phase 12 starter), so the supply-chain surface grows by 0.
- **Left out of the first scope (follow-up)**: Incentive approval concurrency (function-based unique index + contention runner + concurrency IT) follows later, isomorphic to rates (§6.6). For the first scope, the application-layer overlap invariant + in-memory/Oracle contracts suffice.

**Reflected in the design document:** Incorporated directly into `docs/설계서.md` **v1.1.7** — INCENTIVE_MST promoted to the §5.10 body (from the former auxiliary tables), "sandbox mandatory" added to the §3.6 condition expression layer, new Appendix B-16 (sandbox convention for admin-entered expressions), §10 Phase 13 complete.

## 11. Phase 14 (Golden Set CSV Externalization + Preliminary Subtasks) Completion Record — 2026-07-17

Based on design document §8.1, the golden cases were externalized to CSV. 336 tests in total (unit/H2 238 + Oracle integration 98), 0 failures. The output of this Phase is not test code but **a format and guide that settlement staff use directly**.

**Preliminary — breaking down the deferred incentive approval concurrency (split by risk profile):**
- In Phase 13, "incentive approval concurrency as a whole (index + runner + IT)" was deferred as one bundle, but it mixed two parts with different risks. **Done now**: `ux_incentive_active` (V102) — a function-based unique index that indexes only ACTIVE. Even if 2 concurrent approvals both pass the application-layer overlap check, the DB blocks it in the end. Without the index, the worst case of contention is **an overlapping ACTIVE silently appearing, incentive lines being included twice → overpayment**. With the index, the worst case is merely "1 approval fails loudly." **Still deferred**: the retry runner (isomorphic to the rate `OracleRateApprovalRunner`) + deterministic interleaving IT — since the index is already a safety net, this is a convenience (automatic convergence) issue, not a correctness issue.
- **The decisive difference from rates (a pitfall we stepped on directly)**: The incentive target filters (insurer/product/channel) are nullable (NULL = all). Oracle treats NULLs as distinct values in uniqueness comparisons, so indexing the columns as-is, as with rates, lets "two all-target ACTIVEs" **leak through** without colliding. Each filter was wrapped in `NVL('*')` to fix this (handling that was unnecessary for rates because those columns are NOT NULL). The DDL test demonstrates that all-target duplicates are blocked by `UX_INCENTIVE_ACTIVE`.
- **Explicit error mapping (without the retry runner)**: With no runner, an index violation leaks as a raw `DuplicateKeyException` → catch-all → 500. The adapter translates it into `IncentiveApprovalConflictException` (extends IllegalStateException), absorbing it into the existing handler's 409 (STATE_CONFLICT) mapping — 0 handlers or dependencies added. This exception type is also the point where the later runner will catch and retry.
- **Close completeness report extended**: Isomorphic to the rate overlap self-join, it detects incentive overlaps (same `incentive_cd` + NVL-equivalent filters + overlapping periods). The index only blocks "the same start date," so the report acts as a non-blocking safety net catching manual registrations whose periods overlap even with different start dates.

**Golden set CSV — key design judgments:**
- **Self-contained bundles (folder = case)**: Each case holds its entire seed itself. Not relying on a shared seed structurally enforces "when regulations change, add a new case rather than modify existing ones" (the effective-dating philosophy) — fixing a shared seed silently changes past cases, whereas the golden set must pin down the fact that "this is how it was calculated under the regulations at that time." The cost (seed duplication per case) is intentional, and authoring burden is lowered by copying `_TEMPLATE`.
- **Rules = data create the boundaries**: Feature Steps (limit gate, deferral split, etc.) are wired as **always active**, and application and boundaries are driven entirely by the seed rules' `apply_from`. E.g., a policy concluded on 2026-06-30 has no limit applied not because of the Step's activation date but **because the limit rule (effective 2026-07-01) does not exist on the policy date**. Since all boundary cases come from the seed, the cases are truly self-contained.
- **The runner is a thin adapter over the real services**: It assembles `CommissionCalculator`, `RevisionService`, `DeferralReleaseService`, `LimitLedgerService` and `RateApprovalService` as-is and runs the timeline — it does not bypass Steps (completion criterion). Timeline step types: EVENT + operations (APPROVE_RATE · REBOOK · RELEASE · DEDUCT_CLAWBACK).
- **Comparison method**: Lines are a subset (only what is listed is checked) + pre-close corrections use `event=NET` (all-status summation — directly reflecting the §3.0 ban on status-filtered SUM). Schedules are matched exactly as sets. "Something must be absent" (e.g., no clawback at installment 13) is proven through an observable result (ledger unchanged).
- **Refined by repeatedly asking "can a human write this?"**: Seeds were split into **homogeneous tables** per type (the strength of Excel opening them as header + rows). The timeline is kept in one file because order is its essence, and the friction of operation rows leaving many columns empty is cushioned with a `note` column and a README legend. Points not visible at a glance, such as `attrs` (k=v;k=v) and REDUCE's `new_monthly_premium`, are spelled out in the README.

## 12. Phase 15 (Regulatory Add-ons · Migration + Preliminary Correctness Plug) Completion Record — 2026-07-17

Based on design document §1 and §8.5, comparative disclosure, comparative explanation and the shadow run were implemented. Tests went from 336→346 in total (unit/H2 246 + Oracle integration 100), 0 failures.

**Preliminary — "the index guarantees correctness" was an overstatement, and correcting it was the most important decision of this Phase:**
- In Phase 14 we wrote "the index blocks contention, so there is no overpayment risk," but that was wrong. `ux_incentive_active` only adjudicates (key + `apply_from`) collisions, i.e., contention on **the same start date**. If 2 concurrent approvals with different start dates (starting 8/1 and 9/1) pass without seeing each other, an ACTIVE with an overlapping period silently appears, and while the close report is **non-blocking and runs once a month**, calculations run mid-month, leaving a payout window before detection. For rates, the runner's revalidation had absorbed this general contention.
- **A cheap structural plug**: If the rule resolver matches 2 or more rows for the same key/`incentive_cd` on the reference date, it **rejects the calculation with Ambiguous fail-fast** (a natural extension of Appendix B-13). Rates (`findRate`) already behaved that way via `unique()` (in-memory + Oracle contract tests kept), and for incentives `IncentiveV2Step` was extended to group `candidates` by `incentive_cd` and reject when there are 2 or more (in-memory + real-Oracle IT). Now, however an overlap arises, **money cannot move, and the worst case becomes "the calculation fails loudly"** → only then does "no correctness risk" become true. The deferred runner is demoted to pure convenience (automatic convergence), and the spec now nails down **lock-then-revalidate** (for retries to close not only index violations but general overlaps too, the overlap re-check must come after the row lock).

**Comparative disclosure — a structure that does not harden the format by guesswork:**
- The association/authority disclosure format, aggregation unit and submission cycle are **externally determined facts** the system cannot decide, so they were left as §11 #13. Hence a **two-layer design**: the extraction layer (`DisclosureService`) assumes no format and only produces net figures at the finest dimension (close month × insurer × product × recipient × type), and the format mapping layer (`DisclosureFormat`) rolls up and pivots them into a specific format. Tests demonstrate that 2 dummy formats (product × type pivot / insurer totals) produce **different disclosures from the same extracted aggregate** — when the confirmed format arrives, only a mapping implementation is added, without touching extraction or net calculation.
- Insurer and product are not in COMM_CALC but in the source event, so they are tagged by joining on `event_id`. **All net amounts go through `NetAmountCalculator` (all-status summation)** — because disclosures must not double-deduct on pre-close corrections either (the reversal & rebook case is pinned by tests). The comparative explanation rank/grade (`RankingService`) assigns tercile grades on top of this aggregate by (insurer × product) FY net amount, with the calculation logic (data) separated from the display format.

**Shadow run — new module, real Oracle path:**
- No existing module saw all three of calc (net amounts), external CSV import and reconciliation-style categorization at once (api cannot see inbound/recon, recon cannot see calc), so a **new module `commission-shadow`** (→ depends on calc) was added. Regulatory/migration concerns are isolated in an independent module, like recon.
- The comparison axis is, as requested, **recipient × type × close month**. Categories reuse and extend the §7 reconciliation (4 kinds): MATCH / ROUNDING (small difference within the rounding tolerance) / RATE_DIFF (rate difference) / MISSING (missing on our side) / UNEXPECTED (excess on our side) / TIMING (attribution timing). **TIMING is two-pass** — when the same (recipient × type) amount splits into MISSING in one month and UNEXPECTED in another and they cancel out, both are reclassified as TIMING. **Default tolerance is 0 won** (only exact matches are clean), but the rounding tolerance does not hide small differences; it **only classifies** them (everything is reported). Our own net amounts are calculated with `NetAmountCalculator`, and, **unlike the golden set runner (in-memory), the real Oracle COMM_CALC path** is verified by IT (new policies are calculated and saved on real Oracle and then compared with the external settlement, demonstrating per-category classification).

## 15. Phase E3.1 (E3 Acceptance Review Follow-up) Rationale — 2026-09-30

- **Numbering range 1000000–9999999**: If the SEQUENCE started at 1, it could produce the same number on the same day as a `GRD-{날짜}-{6자리}` already issued by the V12 daily counter. Instead of DDL that depends on data (query the maximum, then START WITH), we chose **a dedicated range that cannot overlap** — the same approach as `INCENTIVE_ADMIN_SEQ` (v1.1.3 ②). The ID length is fixed at 20 characters, so `snapshot_id VARCHAR2(20)` does not need to change. It is NOCYCLE, so on exhaustion it fails rather than silently wrapping around (B-13).
- **Unpaired surrogates**: Cross-validating with the ga-disclosure vectors revealed that erdtman 1.1 lets through input that RFC 8785 §3.2.2.2 says MUST be an error. Converting it to UTF-8 as-is replaces it with `?`, silently changing the hash. Engine input is an `ObjectNode` built by the engine, so this does not appear on the real path, but since the premise of seal hash reconciliation is "byte-identical output from the two implementations," it is rejected at the entry point (`Jcs`), aligning the rejection sets of the two repositories as well.
- **Token hash list capped at 2**: The review decision (§4-4) is "two: current and next." An uncapped list becomes a path for old tokens to remain after rotation is finished, so 3 or more is blocked by failing startup.

## 16. Phase E1 (Outbox · Event Feed) — 2026-10-17

**A defect that predated E1, fixed in E1 — deferral cancellation was missing from the operations assembly.** `DeferralCancelListener` (surrender/lapse/withdrawal → remaining schedule CANCELLED) and `DeferralReversalHook` (correction of the original → not-yet-due schedule CANCELLED) existed only in the golden engine and the test assembly, not in the app's `CalcPipelineConfig` or `ServiceConfig`. Even with all 24 golden cases passing, schedules were not cancelled in operations — because the test assembly and the operations assembly were written separately. It surfaced while tracing the emission points backward to attach `DeferralStatusChanged(CANCELLED)` to the outbox. The fix is two lines of assembly, and recurrence is prevented by an **assembly parity test** (`AssemblyParityTest` — calls the app's `@Bean` methods without Spring and compares the class sets of the calculator's post-save hooks and correction reversal hooks with the golden engine's all-features assembly). `DeferralReversalHook` was also missing from the golden engine (added when the deferral feature is on) — none of the deferral cases use REBOOK, so the results of the 24 cases are the same.

**Why not a SEQUENCE — why load order and feed sequence numbers were separated.** The feed consumer (portal) waits on gaps in `seq` as "events that have not arrived yet." Oracle SEQUENCE/IDENTITY discards numbers taken by rolled-back transactions (gaps), and because of caching, commit order and number order can diverge (if a smaller number committed late appears after a larger number already delivered, the consumer skips it). So business transactions load rows without a number (`event_no` is only load order), and numbers are assigned starting at `last_seq + 1` only to committed rows, by locking a one-row sequence table (`OUTBOX_SEQ … FOR UPDATE`). A single lock serializes all assignment, but assignment transactions are short (at most N rows) and business transactions do not take that lock. Assignment is placed both in a write transaction inside the feed call (lazy) and in a job because the engine has no scheduler — portal polling pulls the assignment along. One assumption remains: IDENTITY monotonicity holds only on a single instance (RAC is out of scope, design document §6.8).

**Why a repository decorator rather than a listener.** There are three paths that insert into `COMM_CALC` (calculation, correction reversal, deferral falling due), and only calculation goes through `CalcResultListener`. Had it been attached to the listener, the calculation events from corrections and deferrals falling due would have been missed. Wrapping the port makes "every insert = an event" hold structurally, with business code and Steps unchanged. Only for the two facts not captured by repositories (close confirmation, payout statement) were minimal listener ports added to the business services.

**Defects that predated E1 — surfaced while trying to publish facts externally.**
- **Limit deferral does not defer** (DEFER_AFTER_FY = same behavior as a cut, no schedule). The directive assumed `DeferralScheduled(LIMIT_DEFER)` — a fact the engine does not produce cannot be published. Design document §11 E1-1.
- **Deferral cancellation was missing from the operations assembly** (first paragraph of §16 above). Fixed in E1, pinned by the assembly parity test.
- **Corrections (ADJUSTMENT) are not paid out** — there is no caller, and neither the close nor the payout run reads them. They were put into `StatementConfirmed.adjustment` (a fact of the ledger) but not into `PayoutCompleted` (writing an unpaid value in a payout event would make the portal read it as paid). Design document §11 E1-2.
- **The payout statement (tax amount, net amount) and payout time were not persisted** — they existed only in return values and the batch execution context. `PAYOUT_STATEMENT` (V107) was created as the canonical record (Appendix B-15).
- **`closed_at` was the DB time and was overwritten on CLOSING and OPEN transitions too** — since it must equal the close event's `closedAt`, it was changed to use the injected clock and to be written only on the CLOSED transition (behavior change, design document §7).
- **Nobody writes `LIMIT_LEDGER_HIST`**, **the close-month guard is missing from the operations assembly**, **receivable lookups take no lock** — recorded, not fixed (design document §11 E1-3 to E1-5).
